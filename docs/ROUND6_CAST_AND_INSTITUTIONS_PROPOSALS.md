## 2. Proposed political parties and institutions

Two major parties **exist in canon**. Their suggested labels below are new and unapproved:

- **Common Hearth Party** — welfare spending, civil liberties, limits on emergency rules, equal access to schools and care. Ada Fen is a proposed leader; internal budget factions disagree about migration and taxes.
- **Crown and Order Party** — security, royal continuity, business stability and decisive emergency enforcement. Silas Frost is a proposed leader; some supporters want constitutional limits, others want unlimited discretion.

Specific policies should have real effects on public ledgers and character lives. A public-service policy can be underfunded; security regulations may stop a real criminal threat and also harm innocent families. No party or species should be assigned a blanket moral score.

## 3. Relationship graph that generates behavior (proposed)

1. **Button ↔ Mama:** deep attachment, approval-seeking helpfulness; Mama's work absences produce fear, attempts to follow, and conflict with obligations.
2. **Hazel ↔ Mama:** affection with frequent authority clashes; trust depends on Mama respecting curiosity and establishing consistent boundaries.
3. **Button ↔ Hazel:** *unfixed and bidirectional*: may comfort, play, collaborate, compete, gossip, blame, fight, form grudges, or grow to hate each other. Each remembers concrete past incidents.
4. **Button/Hazel ↔ Grandma:** both hate her **for good reason**. She enjoys threatening, humiliating or coercing unpleasant dares, but it is not safe or neutral play. Mama initially knows Grandma is mean, not necessarily all event details.
5. **Mama ↔ Grandma:** financial/work dependency with knowledge of Grandma's behavior; ordinary alternative childcare requires a substantial solution arc. Urgent protective measures remain possible.
6. **Mama ↔ Oren/Tessa:** performance and trust; a legal exception may help a family while damaging Mama's employment or coworker relationships.
7. **Mama ↔ Vera/Ivo:** authority versus clinical/care judgment; if staff act while Mama is at the gate, outcomes carry over to refuge days.
8. **Hazel ↔ Rook ↔ Ash:** preschool experimentation and exposure to older pupils, without treating young Hazel like a teen. Older network can gradually gain or lose influence.
9. **Button ↔ Fig ↔ Elowen:** friendship, indoor shared projects and teacher observations of distress.
10. **Lorne ↔ Mama ↔ public institutions:** loan leverage, social status and conflict-of-interest risk; criminal pressure can exploit finances without assuming Mama automatically participates.
11. **Claudia ↔ Rina ↔ Ada/Silas:** news claims, sources, verification, leak risk, legal protections, institutional favor.
12. **Dorian ↔ teachers/refuge staff:** justice/welfare referrals, individual authority limits and contestable evidence.

### Mechanical character schema (not coded yet)

Suggested persistent fields:
`id, species, physical_age_days, development_stage, current_household_id, legal_guardian_id, work_role_id, school_group_id, current_location_id, schedule_id, personality_axes, needs, memories[], relations{}, individual_goals[], injuries[], history[], observed_facts[], beliefs[], legal_cases[], timestamps`.

Store **bond, fear, resentment, sympathy and recent conflict** independently; no single "likes" number. Relationship asymmetry is allowed (Button may still seek Hazel's company while Hazel dislikes him, or the reverse). Memories require an initiator, target, location, cause, severity and known witnesses.

## 4. Sibling fights, harm, tears and consequences (approved capabilities, proposed implementation)

At six months the cubs are developmentally preschoolers. Their conflict should arise from needs and context, not a random violence button.

**Possible causes:** fatigue, jealous attachment to Mama, broken possessions, a secret exposed, unequal comfort, being blamed for misbehavior, humiliation in front of classmates, Grandma deliberately provoking them.

**Actions:** warning noises, insults, lying, exclusion, taking an object, pushing, swatting, biting, scratching, defensive retreat, calling Mama, seeking comfort, or escalating. Keep animations anatomically quadrupedal.

**Outcomes:** visible crying with **actual tears** (not generic angry emoji), fear, embarrassment, bruises/minor injuries or more serious injury when circumstances warrant, loss of trust, avoidance, revenge plans, comforting reconciliation, or long-term hatred. A fight does not automatically end in forgiveness.

**Critical rules:**
- Do not make violent injury inevitable every day or reward it as inherently funny. Represent cause and care consequences.
- Damage to emotional trust and physical health is distinct.
- Adult intervention can fail, backfire, or be partial.
- Both children remain active agents with separate interpretation of events.
- Physical harm must remain plausible to age, size and context; no automatic teenage abilities.
- The user expects **harsh possible consequences** but individual serious scenes still need narrative design.
- Bear crying, facial expressions, ear posture and body movement should be supported in the sprite/animation specification.

## 5. Grandma's cruel dares (approved severity; proposed encounter design)

The user approved deliberate cruelty for amusement, including hazing-like coercion and dangerous/humiliating orders (eating insects, unknown liquids, entering lake water, provoking a larger adult). Treat these as **abuse by a caretaker**, not normal discipline, "toughening up", or a comedy minigame. The game can depict the demand and what happened, without simulating real-world dangerous procedures.

A case record should preserve:
- the demand made, its coercive context, who was present, what Grandma claimed afterward;
- whether each cub refused, complied, fled, or sought help (their responses are autonomous);
- witnessed observable behavior, fear/physical state, injuries if any;
- what Mama actually knows versus what happened;
- inquiries, care, reporting, safeguarding, financial/work consequences, and trust changes.

**Example branching vignette:** Grandma challenges the cubs to approach a restricted lake as a "test of bravery." Hazel resists being ordered around, Button worries but fears being mocked. A witness may spot the situation; Mama may learn immediately, from a teacher later, or through an injury/contradiction. The player's task is to respond and investigate; the game does not instruct the child to perform the danger.

The user chose a **substantial storyline** to secure normal alternate childcare, not an instant menu toggle. Potential steps: document mistreatment, build trustworthy contacts, compare cost and availability of caregivers, negotiate work cover, access formal welfare options or establish an emergency arrangement. However **immediate protective action** remains available in an acute crisis; finding *sustainable* replacement care takes time.

Grandma's motive is **enjoyment of cruel behavior**, not merely ignorance. The player may confront or report her, but the story must not insist she is lovable, redeemable, or safe.

## 6. School schedule and observable scenes

Half-day structured preschool, with activities, meals, nap/rest and outdoor activity. **Button and Hazel share a school but have separate teachers and peer graphs.**

**Example phase schedule only (times unapproved):**
- 08:30 arrival, teacher greeting.
- 09:00 indoor activities: Button builds a toy mechanism; Hazel asks why an activity is mandatory.
- 10:00 shared snack with peer-specific interactions.
- 10:30 separate outdoor or supervised exploration.
- 11:15 rest, story, teacher observations.
- 12:30 Grandma collects the cubs; Mama is still at work.

**Current scope approved:** school reports, conversations, observable classrooms and playgrounds during visits/scenes (**B**).
**Later scope requested:** periodic playable preschool perspective (**C later**). Do not present this as already built.

## 7. Profession and institution requirements for the cast

An adult's job is a record with qualifications, hire reason, rank, shift days, workplace contacts, salary, political constraints, performance and disciplinary history. The user approved mixed qualification pathways — exams, apprenticeships, patronage or experience, varying by profession.

- Oren can recommend promotions and issue reports but cannot rewrite court judgments.
- Vera can assign routine refuge care and flag medical issues while Mama is elsewhere; Ivo signs medical evaluations.
- Teachers can issue school reports, remove a child from an activity and refer concerns; **they cannot directly impose a juvenile criminal sentence**.
- Dorian can investigate and recommend juvenile charges within authority; only courts impose sentences when applicable. Age rules vary by offense and emergency directives.
- Party leaders can sponsor or block laws through parliamentary procedures; Queen can suspend most laws indefinitely during emergencies.
- Journalists make *claims based on sources*; those claims can be wrong, corrected, suppressed or disputed.
- Lorne records Mafia debt/collections; no universal panda criminal affiliation.

## 8. Opening-week interactions (design examples, not approved story events)

**Gate day 1:** Oren gives Mama a new emergency directive. Tessa whispers that a stranded family needs help. Claudia's government paper celebrates the "safe city" policy. Grandma picks up the cubs; Button cries, Hazel challenges her. One independently persistent family appears at the gate.

**Refuge day 2:** Vera reports residents' heating shortage; Ivo refuses an unsafe intake plan. Rina requests budget records. Hazel and Rook argue over a school rule; Button tries to build something with Fig.

**Gate day 3:** Tessa sees a traveler from day 1. Oren's performance review may conflict with evidence. Grandma humiliates a cub while concealing it from Mama; partial information reaches school.

**Refuge day 4:** A funding committee with Ada/Silas connections changes grant limits; Mama can seek funds or contest a decision. Button's blunt report creates a sibling dispute.

**Gate day 5:** Lorne contacts Mama about a Honey Marks installment. Rina may have evidence involving a minister. Dorian investigates an older juvenile (not a preschooler) connected to the Old Burrows network.

**Refuge day 6:** Refuge needs a placement decision; Grandma care conflict becomes urgent. School's accounts of the cubs' behavior can be compared with their own, Grandma's and witnesses' versions.

**Off day 7:** Family interactions and unresolved investigations. Player can pursue credible alternate-care plans, social relationships, shopping or rest. Time continues naturally.

## 9. First implementation acceptance tests (not yet run)

1. Spawn 20 core characters with stable unique IDs; no role or name collision replaces another entity.
2. **Legacy v0.1 conflict warning:** The original Briarwood Refuge prototype already contains **a named cub Button** and **an adult bear Hazel**. The **new player family** has canon Button and Hazel. During source import, inventory all legacy IDs and decide whether to adapt, rename or preserve those former NPCs separately; **never assign the player cub IDs by matching display names and never silently erase originals**. Existing saves are not automatically compatible. Keep provenance.
3. With Grandma on duty, Button/Hazel fear and resentment reflect witnessed events and Mama's partial knowledge; the two siblings can react differently and conflict with each other.
4. A school report is an NPC's account, not omniscient truth. A disagreement between reports and events is possible.
5. Sibling argument can cause tears, temporary estrangement or injury; memory persists after reload; reconciliation is not compulsory.
6. Half-day preschool ends with pickup by an actual caregiver who traveled there, not teleporting all people by scene switch.
7. Gate/refuge/weekly-off rotation persists; shift timer can pause/disable without erasing progress or advancing NPC ages incorrectly.
8. Queen Kodiak brown bear and other bears use quadrupedal animations; tears are visible in appropriate emotion states.
9. Post-martyrdom selects **exactly one** main playable sibling while the other keeps an autonomous state; prior relationships and injuries survive.
10. Repeatably visiting a district never spawns a duplicate copy of the same recurring character.
